# solid-kernel

> TL;DR: B-rep solids for the FEA preprocessor (ADR-019). Always built: `SurfaceSolid` (a closed, outward, face-tagged
> surface mesh with BVH signed-distance and per-face distance queries) and `mesh_volume` (tetrahedra through
> `fea-core::tetmesh`). Feature `occt` (off by default; the `cadrum` crate links a prebuilt static OpenCASCADE 8.0.1, no C++
> toolchain; set `OCCT_ROOT` to an extracted tarball if its build-script download fails): `occt::Brep` (extrude a sketch profile, union / cut / intersect,
> fillet, STEP in and out, face-tagged tessellation).

## Purpose
Owns: the surface-mesh solid and its distance queries, the OpenCascade modelling wrapper, the tessellation into face-tagged triangles.
Does not own: the volume mesher (`fea-core/src/tetmesh.rs`), the problem schema (`fea-problem`), any UI.

## Contracts
- A `SurfaceSolid` is only built from a **closed manifold** (every edge in exactly two triangles, opposite directions); an open or
  non-manifold tessellation is refused with a reason, an inside-out one is turned around.
- The sign of the distance uses angle-weighted pseudo-normals, so it is right at edges and vertices.
- Face tags are B-rep face indices in the kernel's face order; the mesher names the faces `face{tag + 1}`.
- The tessellation deflection bounds the geometric error: use a small fraction of the boundary element size.

## Pitfalls
- A spline-surface face closed in one parameter (an OpenCASCADE fillet ring) has two chart images for a point on its seam, and a nearest-point search alone picks either: project a boundary loop with `SplineSurface::chart_loop` (continuation), never sample by sample with `closest`. OpenCASCADE's intersection curves lie ~1e-5 mm off the fillet surface, so those files need `geometry_tolerance_mm: 1e-4` (never loosen it further to pass). A 2 x 2 bilinear spline net stays planar-patch-only (a warped one is refused). B-spline edge and swept curves are read on their active domain (`read_curve_active_domain`: OpenCASCADE's periodic splines are unclamped with extended controls, nothing is wrapped); B-spline surfaces too (`NurbsSurface::new_active_domain`, point evaluation only: derivatives and enclosures stay clamped-only). A revolution's parameters are (angle, curve) and an extrusion's (curve, height): STEP's order fixes the normal, so swapping them flips it and the shell fails the orientation check.
- Plain per-face native sampling leaves needles on most faces (sparse boundaries, no interior points): use `AnalyticModel::graded` + `Limits::chart_quality` before `plc_conform` (graded generic parts reach ~25 degrees and a radius-edge ratio of 2; plain ones are refused). What grading cannot fix is near-degenerate CAD (the bracket's 5 micrometre sliver faces): that needs B-rep healing, and shell-level `shell_defeature` rejects the clusters of a graded shell. Grading keeps the chart's split fractions (their concentric-shell placement is what grades toward small features: bisecting every split left the bracket over its needle gate) but drops a new edge sample nearer than the model's geometry tolerance to another sample of its segment; spline intervals are bisected. Without that, two faces' copies of each split and small-angle cascades left samples nanometres apart (ABC `00006600`, Phase 157). The `floor_mm` check is on the world chord: sphere and torus charts are conformal, not millimetres.
- `plc_conform` quality refinement: the cheap recipe is a graded shell, `protect_encroached: false`, `max_radius_edge: Some(2.)` (radius-edge ~2 with 10x fewer cells); protect-everything cascades at acute (< 90 degree) wedges and now stops at `min_segment_relative`. Circumcentres are inserted only strictly inside their cell's exact circumsphere.
- `plc_conform` converges for well-conditioned PLCs (boxes, plates with holes) but NOT for sampled surface meshes with skinny triangles such as the NEMA17 bracket: every triangle edge is a protected segment and Delaunay refinement cascades past any budget (it refuses with a `Limit` error in ~2 s). Do not raise `max_steiner` to compensate; the fix is TetGen-style boundary recovery by local reconnection, or an isotropic surface remesh first. `shell_patch::dissolve_flat` (also `step-inventory --clean-shell`) removes redundant flat/collinear vertices but cannot dissolve a vertex shared with curved triangles at a tangent seam, which is where the remaining bracket needles are. `shell_weld::weld_short_edges` collapses short needle edges safely; what was left there, sliver faces, `shell_defeature` now removes by welding whole cross-section groups; the remaining bracket needles lie along a thin curved band; opt-in `shell_refine` fixes chords on flat faces but cannot raise a face's own input angle. Measured boundary (Phase 83, `tests/plc_angle_sweep.rs`): plain recovery terminates on simple/surface-mesh-like slabs down to <1 degree, but `max_radius_edge = 2` refinement fails at 18 degrees and below and succeeds at 22+; the smallest input angle is not a predictor for recovery alone.
- `cadrum`'s `Face::id` is not unique (an extrusion's two caps share one) and is session-local (not kept by STEP). `tessellate`
  splits a shared id into connected pieces and matches them to faces by centre; a face without triangles makes it fail
  instead of guessing. Never use the kernel id as a persistent face name.
- Meshing a solid thinner than about two boundary elements is not resolved (see `fea-core/src/tetmesh.rs`).
- The `occt` feature downloads or needs a ~30 MB prebuilt OCCT: keep it opt-in so default builds and tests stay offline. ADR-019 addendum records why `cadrum` replaced `opencascade`.

- **Tessellation/welding correction (2026-10-15)**: welding checks Euclidean distance in neighbouring grid cells, not bucket
  equality, and volume integration uses a translated origin. `occt::Brep::tessellate` locally splits coincident interior
  curved-face chords by projecting their midpoints onto the exact trimmed kernel face; face tags and manifold acceptance
  remain intact. The NEMA17 fixture needs eight extra triangles; global very fine tessellation was prohibitively expensive.
  Kernel validity alone does not prove a particular triangle tessellation is a closed manifold.

- Face bound labels are not roles: Onshape (the ABC dataset) writes the hole loops of a face as `FACE_OUTER_BOUND` too, so a face may declare several outer bounds. Roles come from the loop geometry only (`planar_step::classify`, `cylindrical_step`); the declared-outer consistency check applies only to a single declared outer. `examples/abc-corpus.rs` surveys a directory of STEP files (models stay outside the repo, licence stays with their creators); results in `docs/issue-46-abc-corpus-2026-10-18.md`.
- Foreign CAD files need three reader accommodations, all in `AnalyticModel::read`: `read_all` (one model per `MANIFOLD_SOLID_BREP`; `read` still refuses a multi-body document), `seam_unseamed_cylinder_bands` (a cylinder or cone face bounded by two circles gets a generator seam; a vertex is inserted on the second circle and its edge split in every face when the circles start at different angles; synthetic edges have `RawEdge::curve == 0` and ids above the document's largest entity id, so never look them up in the `Inventory`), and pole `VERTEX_LOOP` bounds on spheres/cones are skipped in `step_topology`. `Options::foreign_cad(chord)` sets the edge/surface tolerance to chord / 10 (the default 1e-7 mm refuses 54 of 300 ABC files for edge/surface disagreement; it moves files past the reader, not yet to a conforming mesh); rounded coordinates also need `ChartPolicy::CanonicalizeWithinTolerance` (strict refuses a seam that closes to 1e-12 only, even on an OpenCASCADE fixture). Torus and revolution bands are seamed too (line below).
- `plc_conform` keeps `enc_tris` (sub-facets a vertex encroaches) incrementally: after a vertex goes into a facet mesh only the new sub-facets (corner = that vertex) are checked against all vertices (`refresh_mesh_around`); `forced_tris` marks entries queued for another reason (missing from the Delaunay triangulation, or encroached by a rejected circumcentre) which a refresh drops. A debug-build assertion compares the incremental set with the full recomputation after every refresh: run the conform tests in the debug profile (`cargo test -p solid-kernel --no-default-features --test plc_conform --test plc_angle_sweep -- --include-ignored`) after touching this bookkeeping. Profiling a slow conform: `sample <pid> 10` on macOS, then look at the top of stack. Three caches keep it near-linear and must stay exact: `FacetMesh::spheres` / `ball_box` (circumsphere of each sub-facet and a box around all balls, cleared by every change of `tris`: a new place that edits `tris` must clear `spheres`; a debug assertion compares the cache with a recomputation), `PointGrid` (uniform grid that only prunes candidates for "any point inside this ball"; the caller's exact predicate decides, `None` means ball too large, scan linearly) and the early-out in `has_boundary_edge`. All three were verified to give identical Steiner / cell counts on the ABC corpus (`docs/issue-46-abc-corpus-2026-10-18.md` step 8). `Options::merge_coplanar` (off by default; harness `--merge`) merges adjacent unfrozen coplanar input triangles into one `FacetMesh::patch`: the edges inside a patch are not segments, the patch triangulation is made Delaunay by flips, and the output facets carry as parent the input triangle that contains their centroid (`containing_facet`), so a sub-facet may straddle a removed diagonal; a patch is not reproduced triangle by triangle, so do not turn it on where a caller needs the exact input triangles (`conform_preserving` keeps frozen facets out of patches). `coplanar_groups` has two tolerances (pair 1e-9, whole patch 1e-8) and falls back to singletons; a patch that cannot be built (not consistently oriented) falls back to its triangles. 35 % of ABC floor failures were diagonals of planar faces; the rest are near-flat creases of curved faces and acute dihedrals, which merging does not address.
- Reader/sampling facts from the ABC corpus (Phase 120, `docs/issue-46-abc-corpus-2026-10-18.md`): `SplineSurface` search grids are refined per direction (screw threads alias a fixed grid) and `candidates` repeats a search that ends above 1e-4 mm from grid local minima and, above 5e-3 mm, from `cell_starts` (best-first bilinear-patch subdivision): never replace it by "nearest nodes only". An extrusion's height range is relative to its curve (`swept_surface`). `Options::foreign_cad` tolerance is chord / 10 (measured distribution in its doc comment). `VERTEX_LOOP` bounds are skipped on any face that keeps an edge bound. Two-loop band faces get a synthetic seam in `AnalyticModel::read`: cylinders / cones a generator (`seam_unseamed_cylinder_bands`, edges of any curve kind can be split, extra non-winding bounds stay holes), tori a circle arc (`seam_unseamed_torus_bands` / `torus_seam`; the arc is chosen by the side the face is on, a wrong side is refused by the shell orientation check, so keep that check). Synthetic seam edges have `RawEdge::curve == 0`. A seam end inserted on a loop's edge drops an interior sample of that edge lying within the geometry tolerance of it (`drop_samples_beside`, Phase 162: one such sample gave ABC `00006600` a 4.7 degree shell corner); an end vertex that near is not handled, because taking the vertex puts the seam off the generator and the cone chart refuses it.
- `plc_conform` fallback (Phase 120): when the protect-everything recovery fails with the Steiner-budget or feature-size-floor refusal (or reaches `protected_max_steiner`, 30 000), `conform` repeats once with `protect_encroached: false` (`Options::unprotected_fallback`, default on; turn it off to test the protected rule alone, as `tests/plc_merge.rs` does). Protected-first keeps the better radius-edge ratio (median 2 against 10) and makes every previously conforming result identical. The floor/cascade on sampled CAD shells is driven by rejected circumcentres of needle sub-facets, not by a vertex, so it has no scale to detect: do not try to cut it by a scale criterion (measured). `SegmentGrid` (multi-level, only prunes; debug assertion compares with the full scan) answers "which segments does this point encroach"; any new place that adds or removes a segment must update it. Coordinates below 1e-30 are flushed to 0 at entry (the exact predicates refuse them). Debug tooling: `examples/plc-run.rs` (`PLC_MERGE`, `PLC_NOPROTECT`, `PLC_NOFALLBACK`, `PLC_SECS`; the sliver recipe `PLC_REFINE` with `PLC_QSEC`, `PLC_PASSES`, `PLC_SMOOTH`; `PLC_CELLS` lists each cell under scaled Jacobian 0.1 with its vertex kinds; `PLC_VERIFY_KEPT` and `STAGEDBG` are read by `plc_conform` itself).
- `plc_conform` quality refinement is opt-in (`Options::min_scaled_jacobian`); it inserts bounded Steiner points and may leave unresolved sub-gate cells, counted in `Report::unresolved_bad_cells`. It never converts a conforming mesh into a solver-quality certificate: call `Conforming::to_solver_mesh` for that gate. `examples/abc-corpus.rs --quality` records the gate and a static solve, and `--refine` exercises the opt-in sliver recipe. The 2026-10-20 300-file survey found 3 of 150 returned conforming files passed without refinement, versus 30 of 149 with refinement and 14 timeouts (six more than without). Refused optional quality splits must stay out of both the incremental encroachment queue and its debug invariant, including after later vertex insertions. A segment split that recovers a required (missing) sub-facet is required itself: refusing it at the sliver floor requeued the sub-facet forever (Phase 158). The edge and face presence table (`State::presence`) is updated incrementally by `State::insert`; any new way of changing the triangulation must update it too, or the exact presence check goes stale (Phase 159). Phase 160 keeps more state per insertion, all exact and checked every round with `PLC_VERIFY_KEPT=1` (`plc-run`): the missing sub-segments and sub-facets (`State::missing`, fed by `Presence::count` transitions and by `State::mesh_changed`), the inside flags and badness keys per Delaunay id (`State::cell_cache`, fed by `Delaunay3::take_created`), the facet ball index (`FacetIndex`, `FacetMesh::ball_grid`). Every change of a facet mesh must go through `State::mesh_changed` (or the flip loop's equivalent), and every change of `FacetMesh::tris` must leave `spheres` shorter than `tris` (clear or keep only a prefix of survivors): `ensure_spheres` rebuilds the ball grid only then. The cavity search uses `sphere_side_positive` (insphere alone), so every `Delaunay3` cell must stay positively oriented. The sliver stage's rollback copy (`State::rollback_copy`) leaves out the caches that rebuild from scratch when absent; a new cache that cannot rebuild itself must not be left out. The sliver floor (`optional_floor`) is the shortest input segment of the whole shell, so a shell fix that removes one short piece raises it everywhere: Phase 162 raised it on ABC `00006600` from 6.47e-4 to at least 8.89e-3 mm and left a sliver at an input vertex whose 7.81e-3 mm sub-segments it may no longer split. `abc-corpus --refine` alternates the flip pass with two rounds of interior smoothing (`Options::smooth_rounds`), which moves such a cell's quality point; with it `00006600` passes the gate and solves (Phase 163).
