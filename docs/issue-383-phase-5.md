# Issue 383, Phase 5: public benchmark and output tiers

2026-11-27. [Kraus, Klaus and Wittor (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1002/cepa.1273) adds a stronger direct-tapped comparison than the sources in Phases 1–4. It does not reverse Item 1's no-complete-public-match disposition.

## Kraus Table 3 evidence

The paper reports M12/M20 class 10.9 bolts in S235/S355 tapped blind holes at three effective engagements per combination: 12 groups, five tests each, 60 tests total. Its Table 3 gives each group's mean maximum load, standard deviation, and counts of thread failure versus bolt rupture. These 12 rows are transcribed into `app-tui/src/gpu_viewer/editor/thread_evidence/kraus-blind.json`, converted from mm/kN to inches/lbf with 25.4 mm/in and 4.4482216152605 N/lbf, and shown as immutable source values beside saved editable values. The chart plots mean load against effective engagement with ±one reported standard deviation. The failure counts are source observations and remain read-only. No individual test loads or traces are reconstructed from group statistics.

The tested parent plates came from the same charges and underwent tensile tests. The paper reports parent yield/ultimate strengths and bolt ultimate strengths, but no specimen-level thread profiles, measured full bolt and parent true stress–plastic-strain histories, or independent fracture calibration. The paper notes that measured S235 tensile properties significantly exceed nominal values, so nominal S235/S355 properties are not interchangeable with the tested material. Its simplified 2D FE example is a qualitative comparison, not independent validation of this app's solver. Kraus's metric thread forms and steel conditions also differ from the current inch-only Unified Workbench and its default 1/4-28 case. A future metric solver benchmark needs its own geometry and provenance; converting display units does not convert thread form.

Kraus compares DIN EN 1993-1-8/NA, VDI 2230, and Schwarz/Dose. The paper finds that VDI 2230 tends to underestimate required engagement for these specimens. Therefore VDI is a comparison model, not an exact target for first-turn load share, stiffness, or capacity. Sopwith's analytical load sharing addresses bolt/nut elastic distribution; Alexander's 1977 method addresses bolt/nut ultimate strength and failure mode. Neither alone proves a direct-tapped 3D stress field or plastic collapse load. The proposed 2–5% and ±12% targets are hypotheses until measured on defined benchmark sets and compared with reported scatter.

## Product output boundary

The Thread Workbench now labels its current results as an **analytical strength screen** and **1D elastic FE load sharing**. The tapped summary explicitly states that plastic-collapse and progressive-stripping capacities are unavailable. The separate offline axisymmetric contact pilot has elastic and finite-strain J2 diagnostics, but its yielding-parent mesh gate is unresolved; it has no material deletion or validated failure capacity. No fixed knockdown factor can repair unknown model-form error. Nut tests can exercise bolt/nut failure code once a comparable formulation exists, but cannot transfer fracture parameters into a different tapped parent.

Before adding a plastic-collapse limit to runtime, define a reproducible limit-load or residual-distortion observable, validate its material inputs and boundary conditions, and pass load-step/mesh/contact convergence. Label it as a model-based onset estimate until direct-tapped comparison evidence supports its accuracy. Before adding progressive stripping, independently calibrate the regularized fracture law and report its held-out error and failure-mode agreement on a dataset with enough specimen detail. Do not publish a scatter band derived from group means, nominal grades, or a model used for tuning.
