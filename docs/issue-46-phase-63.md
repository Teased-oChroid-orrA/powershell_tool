# Issue 46 — Phase 63: metric-driven refinement (first step of #58)

Stacked on Phase 62 (PR #89).

`fea-core::meshing::metric_refine::refine(topology, field, bounds, options)` drives a tetrahedral mesh toward a metric field `Fn(point) -> Metric`: every edge longer than `max_length` (default sqrt 2) in the metric is split at its midpoint, then the cells are reconnected by the exact 2-3 / edge-removal flips of Phase 60, now judged by the **radius ratio in the cell's mean metric** (`tet_flip::improve_by` takes any quality function). Splitting follows Rivara's terminal-edge rule (walk to the longest metric edge of the cells around a candidate until it is the longest in all of them; independent terminal edges are split per pass) so each split halves a longest edge. The field is validated at every vertex, new ones included, against the `MetricBounds`; a violation, a cell-budget overrun and a non-finite metric are errors. Splitting every cell around an edge keeps the mesh conforming and the polyhedral boundary exactly the same surface; vertices never move, nothing is removed, and boundary points are not projected onto a curved CAD surface.

Evidence: a cube refined to an isotropic size of 0.25 converges (longest edge 4.37 down to sqrt 2 or less), keeps volume 1 and boundary area 6 to 1e-12, and lands within a factor 3 of the closed-form complexity prediction; a size-0.1-to-0.4 layer (anisotropy 4) around the plane x = 0.5 converges, concentrates vertices there and makes the cells hugging the layer thinner across it than along it; strong anisotropy (ratio 10) still yields a valid conforming mesh with exact volume and boundary but not good shapes. A coarse field changes nothing; invalid, out-of-bounds and NaN fields and the cell budget are refused. `fea-core` meshing tests pass.

Limits (found while building it): without edge collapse, smoothing or metric-space Delaunay insertion the cell quality degrades under strong anisotropy; an earlier midpoint-only version without the terminal-edge rule drove the minimum radius ratio to 1e-19 and did not converge, which is why the rule is in. Anisotropic adaptation of an existing production mesh also needs coarsening, which this does not do, and a curved-boundary projection. Not claimed: #58's exit (a unit-metric anisotropic mesh), integration with the AMR controller, or solver benefit.
