# Issue 46 — Phase 76: cluster-weld defeaturing of sliver faces (#52/#53)

`solid_kernel::shell_defeature::defeature_slivers(vertices, facets, options)` removes sliver faces (strips a hair wide along a long edge) from any closed, outward-oriented triangulated shell. It is the answer to the blocker Phase 70 measured: a short edge across the strip cannot be collapsed (its endpoints are also joined by the long rails, so the link condition fails), but the vertices of a whole cross-section can be welded together.

- A *cluster* is a connected set of vertices joined by edges (no longer than `max_edge_relative` x bounding-box diagonal, default 1e-3) of a needle triangle (smallest corner below 5 degrees), whose pairwise distance never exceeds that length. Each cluster collapses onto its medoid, so geometry moves by at most that length.
- Clusters that share a triangle form a *group* (the stations of one strip) and a group is welded **as one transaction**: welding a single station leaves long needles to its neighbours (measured, 2e-5 degrees), which the angle guard rightly refuses.
- The weld drops triangles with two equal vertices, cancels two triangles on the same vertices with opposite orientation (the two faces of the former strip), and removes the zero-area *caps* it can leave (three exactly collinear points) by flipping the cap's longest edge with the triangle across it.
- A group is kept only if the shell is still a closed oriented 2-manifold with no zero-area facet, the signed volume moved by at most four times the weld length times the surface area, and the smallest corner angle of the triangles around the welded vertices is not worse than `min(before, acceptable_angle_deg = 10)`. Rejections are counted by reason in the report. Self-intersections are not tested here (the displacement is bounded by the weld length; the checked-PLC admission before constrained recovery detects them).
- `prepare_shell` now runs weld, defeaturing and flat-patch cleanup (cleanup tolerances widened to twice the longest displacement of either stage).

Evidence (macOS arm64): 4 unit tests. A square prism with a 1e-3 chamfer (smallest angle < 1 degree) is welded to a clean prism; a chamfer strip with a mid-width vertex and four stations along its length (smallest angle 0.0287 degrees, 24 vertices, 44 facets) becomes 16 vertices, 28 facets and a smallest angle of 44.99 degrees, with 4 clusters applied and 0 rejected and the volume within the bound; a healthy box is untouched and an open shell is refused; the result is deterministic and never has a worse minimum angle. `solid-kernel` suite: 205 passed, 0 failed; clippy clean for the new file.

Sampled bracket shell (an example only): 8 clusters found in one group, applied together: 48 degenerate triangles removed, 4 coincident sheets cancelled, 2 caps flipped, 465 to 445 vertices, 970 to 914 facets, longest weld 5.4e-3 mm, and the smallest shell angle rises from **0.0123 to 0.735 degrees (60 times)** with the shell still closed. The registry still answers `ShellAngleTooSmall` (the audited bound is 18 degrees) and constrained recovery still exhausts a 3000-point Steiner budget.

Limits (not fixed): the remaining worst triangles of the sample are long chords across coarsely sampled arcs on flat faces (for example a 30 mm base whose apex is 0.7 mm from one end, 0.735 degrees): the polygon itself turns by under a degree at those vertices, so no triangulation without extra points avoids a thin triangle there. That needs surface Steiner refinement (isotropic remeshing of flat faces under their boundary polygon), which is not implemented. The cluster extent and the needle threshold are heuristics with documented defaults; curved patches are not remeshed; no Windows or hosted-CI evidence.
