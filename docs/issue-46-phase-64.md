# Issue 46 — Phase 64: rigorous validity of curved quadratic elements (#55)

Stacked on Phase 63 (PR #90).

`fea-core::meshing::curved::simplex_validity(kind, nodes, max_depth)` decides whether a Tet10 or Tri6 isoparametric map has a positive Jacobian over the whole element, not only at its corners. The determinant is a cubic (Tri6: quadratic) polynomial in the barycentric coordinates; its Bernstein-Bezier coefficients, obtained exactly by solving the collocation system on the degree-3 (2) lattice, bound it from below. All coefficients positive proves the element `Valid`; a non-positive polynomial value at a lattice point proves it `Invalid`; otherwise the simplex is subdivided (8 sub-tetrahedra / 4 sub-triangles) to `max_depth`, leaving `Undetermined` if still unresolved. Values are the ratio `det J / det J_straight`, so a straight element reads exactly 1. `check_mesh` applies it to every curved block and reports valid / invalid / undetermined counts, the first invalid element and the smallest proven bound.

**A real hole closed.** The signed-distance mesher's own curving guard (`tetmesh::tet10_ok`) tested the Jacobian at 11 fixed points. A random search over bent elements finds about 1 in 200 that pass those samples but fold between them (1043 of 200 000 in the study run); `tet10_ok` now also requires the rigorous `Valid` verdict (depth 5), and a regression test finds sample-blind elements by the same search and checks they are refused. No existing mesh changed: all `tetmesh` tests and the full `fea-core` suite pass (478 passed, 22 ignored).

Evidence: straight elements read exactly 1 under any affine map; a Tri6 bent inward by 0.15 has minimum 0.4 (analytic `1 - 4 d xi`) found as both bound and sample, outward bend stays at 1; 200 random bent Tet10s agree with a degree-24 brute-force lattice in both directions (Valid implies positive brute minimum and bound below it; Invalid has a non-positive sampled value); a folded and an inverted-corner element are `Invalid`, a non-finite one and Tet4 are errors. On the SDF-meshed unit sphere (quadratic) all 1824 and 3840 elements are proved valid with minimum bound 0.12 and 0.10, and a deliberately displaced midside node is located by `check_mesh`.

Finding (not fixed): the curved sphere's volume error is about three times smaller than the straight-sided mesh's (4.4e-3 vs 1.4e-2 at h = 0.5) but does not fall monotonically with h (4.4e-3, 4.2e-3, then 8.8e-4 at h = 0.2), because boundary nodes end up to 3 % of the radius off the surface (worst |r-1| 3.2e-2 at h = 0.5, 1.4e-2 at h = 0.2): more snap passes (6 vs 20) change nothing, and the chord sagitta h^2/8 = 0.031 at h = 0.5 equals the worst deviation, so the straight midside nodes whose full curving the validity guard refuses (and the corners beyond reach) are the cause. Exact CAD-parameter projection with validity-guarded steps, high-order convergence studies and assembly cost remain open for #55.
