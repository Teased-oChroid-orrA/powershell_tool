# Issue 46 — Phase 78: verified prism-to-tetrahedron transition (#56)

`fea_core::meshing::transition::tetrahedralize_wedges(mesh, select)` converts selected `Wedge6` elements of any three-dimensional mesh into `Tet4` elements, so that a swept (prismatic) region meets a tetrahedral one through a conforming interface (the epic's "prism–tet" transition; the 2D quad–triangle hybrid already existed in `meshing::hybrid`).

Method: each selected wedge is cut into three tetrahedra. Its three vertical quad faces are each split by the diagonal that contains the smallest of the quad's four **global node numbers**; both elements sharing a quad derive the same diagonal from the same four numbers, so adjacent converted wedges are cut identically, and a converted wedge meets an unconverted one only through a triangular face, which tetrahedra and wedges share exactly. The two quads containing the wedge's smallest node both take their diagonal from it, so the three diagonals can never cycle and the split always exists: that node is the apex of a pyramid over the opposite quad (two tetrahedra, cut by the opposite quad's own diagonal) plus one tetrahedron over the remaining triangle. Every tetrahedron is oriented positively. Boundary surfaces keep their names and their quad faces on converted wedges become the two triangles of the same cut, orientation preserved; node sets, materials, density and thermal data carry over (the tetrahedra form a new block `<name>-tet`).

Refusals (the epic's "verified fallback rather than silently inserting invalid interfaces"): a selection that separates two wedges sharing a *quad* face is an error naming the face; second-order wedges (`Wedge15` would need `Tet10`: orders are not mixed), non-3D meshes and a result with a non-positive scaled Jacobian are errors.

Evidence (macOS arm64): 4 unit tests. **Exhaustive:** for all 720 numberings of the six nodes of a prism the split is three non-flat tetrahedra of total volume 1/2. On a 3x3x4 swept unit cube whose top layers (centroid z > 0.5) are converted: `tets_created = 3 x wedges_converted`, total volume unchanged to 1e-12, `check_conformity` clean (no mismatched or non-manifold faces, no hanging nodes), every boundary surface keeps its area (quads on converted layers became triangle pairs), positive scaled Jacobian. **Patch test:** the mixed Wedge6/Tet4 mesh with a linear displacement prescribed on its whole boundary reproduces the field at every interior node to 1e-10, which a non-conforming interface (a quad paired with two triangles, or an inconsistent cut) cannot. A split-in-plane selection, a 2D mesh, and the identity selection behave as stated. `cargo test -p fea-core`: see the PR.

Limits (not fixed): only the prism-tet pair (first order); the hex-wedge, hex-pyramid-tet and quad-tet combinations of the epic are not provided (the kernel has no pyramid element, and hex-tet needs either one or a boundary-constrained tetrahedral mesher on the hex side's triangulation); the converted region is a whole-wedge layer (the caller selects it), so choosing where to transition by quality or cost is left to the caller or to `meshing::cost`; contact tags and history variables are not remapped (a mesh with plasticity data is copied block-wise but the history is not transferred); no Windows or hosted-CI evidence.
