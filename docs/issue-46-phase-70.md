# Issue 46 — Phase 70: manifold-safe weld of near-degenerate short shell edges (#52/#53)

Stacked on Phase 69 (PR #96).

New `solid_kernel::shell_weld::weld_short_edges(vertices, facets, options)` for any closed, outward-oriented triangulated shell. Candidates are edges no longer than `max_edge_relative` (1e-3) x the bounding-box diagonal that belong to a triangle with a corner below `bad_angle_deg` (5), thinnest first. A collapse `u -> v` is applied only if the link condition holds (the common neighbours of `u` and `v` are exactly the two apexes of the edge, so the shell stays a 2-manifold), every changed triangle keeps positive area and its normal within `max_normal_change_deg` (30), and the smallest changed corner angle does not fall below `min(old, acceptable_angle_deg = 10)`; both directions are tried and the better kept. Collapses in one round touch disjoint stars; rounds repeat. The result must be a closed oriented manifold with no zero-area facet and a volume within `4 x longest collapsed edge x surface area` of the input. The report counts rejections by reason (link, normal, angle, busy). `step-inventory --clean-shell` now runs the weld, then the Phase 69 flat-patch cleanup, before `--conform`.

Evidence (macOS arm64): 5 unit tests. A grid box whose top-edge point is pulled to 2e-4 from its end and 2e-6 out of the wall plane (smallest angle 0.0058 degrees, not dissolvable by any flat patch) welds with one collapse to 18.44 degrees (atan(1/3), the best for the remaining triangles), valid closed shell, volume within 1e-3; healthy shells and over-long edges are untouched; deterministic and never worse; an open shell is refused. `solid-kernel` suite (feature off): 198 passed, 0 failed; clippy clean for the new file. Sampled bracket shell (an example only): 4 collapses (the longest 5.4e-3 mm), 469 to 465 vertices, 978 to 970 facets, every one of the other candidates correctly refused.

**Why the remaining needles stay (measured, not fixed).** 168 candidates are refused by the link condition and none by normal or angle. A diagnostic on three of them shows a separating 3-cycle: points p1, r1, q1 at one end of a 50 mm x 0.0107 mm strip are joined by the chord p1-q1 and by the arc p1-r1-q1, and r1 is 4e-6 mm from the chord (a fillet of about 3.6 mm radius spanning 0.17 degrees). Collapsing r1 onto q1 would glue the two sides and pinch the shell, so the refusal is right. Removing those needles needs defeaturing: deleting the sliver face and stitching its neighbours. The smallest angle of the sample therefore stays 0.0123 degrees and `--conform` still exhausts its budget.

Limits: no sliver-face removal (above); the angle and length thresholds are heuristics with documented defaults; curved patches are not remeshed; no Windows or hosted-CI evidence; not wired into production (OCCT stays production).

**Measurement for #55 (recorded, not fixed).** On the signed-distance unit sphere (`Solid` with `sdf = |x| - 1`, linear tetrahedra) the mesher's report gives `snapped / refused` boundary vertices of 194 / 96 (h = 0.5), 425 / 99 (h = 0.3) and 1658 / 192 (h = 0.2): 10-33 % of the boundary vertices stay off the surface because the snap quality guards (`MIN_QUALITY`, `MAX_QUALITY_LOSS`) refuse the move, not because of the reach or pass count. A fix has to reshape the boundary layer (for example remove or collapse refused vertices, or smooth the layer under a validity guard); it is a separate experiment.
