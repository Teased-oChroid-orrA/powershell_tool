# Issue 46 — Phase 77: shape-preserving surface Steiner refinement of a shell (#53)

`solid_kernel::shell_refine::refine_surface(vertices, facets, options)` adds points to a closed, outward-oriented triangulated shell without changing its shape. A triangle whose smallest corner is below `target_angle_deg` (20) has its Rivara terminal edge bisected at the midpoint (walk across longest edges until both triangles agree, then split the edge and the neighbour across it, so the shell stays conforming); after each round, edges between **coplanar** triangles (the opposite vertex within `coplanar_tolerance` x diagonal of the plane) are flipped when they violate the Delaunay condition and the quad is strictly convex (a work queue, not a rescan). Midpoints lie on existing straight edges and flips stay within one plane, so the surface, the volume and the area are unchanged (re-checked to 1e-9; a violation is an error). It stops at the target, at a budget of `max_new_vertex_factor` x the input vertex count (default 4, at least 16 vertices), or when a round makes no progress, and the report states `reached_target` truthfully together with both the smallest corner angle and the registry's smallest *input* angle (corner or dihedral) before and after.

`prepare_shell_with(..., Some(options))` runs it as an opt-in last stage (`step-inventory --refine-shell`); `prepare_shell` does not, because it adds vertices.

Evidence (macOS arm64): 3 unit tests. A 10 x 10 x 10 prism whose top and bottom faces contain a needle (a vertex bent 0.05 out of a 10 mm edge, smallest angle 0.57 degrees) is refined with 2 new vertices and 2 flips to 26.57 degrees, shape unchanged (volume and area to 1e-9, original vertices untouched); a healthy box is left alone; an open shell is refused; zero rounds or a starved budget leave a valid shell and report `reached_target = false`; the result is deterministic and never lowers the shell angle. `solid-kernel` suite: see the PR; clippy clean for the new file.

Defects found and fixed while building it (each was a wrong result, not a style point): (1) the new vertex index double-counted the vertices added in the round and indexed out of bounds; (2) the convexity test of the flipped quad had both cross products reversed, so no flip ever fired and refinement made the sample worse (0.735 to 0.443 degrees, 1676 points added, 0 flips); (3) bisecting each bad triangle's own longest edge converged badly and the full flip rescan was quadratic (a 40x budget run did not finish in ten minutes), so the terminal-edge rule and the queue replaced them.

**Measured on the sampled bracket shell (an example only), not solved.** After weld, defeaturing and cleanup the smallest angle is 0.735 degrees. With `--refine-shell` it reaches 1.446 degrees with the default budget (1676 points added, 985 flips) and stays at 1.446 degrees with a 40x budget (16,760 points added, 11,292 flips): the limiting triangles lie along what is left of the former strip, where the surface is a thin curved band whose straight chords cannot be improved by points on them. The registry therefore still answers `ShellAngleTooSmall` (gate 18 degrees) and constrained recovery still exceeds its budget.

Limits (not fixed): refinement works inside a face under its fixed boundary polygon and on straight edges; a face whose own boundary edges meet at a small angle, or a thin curved band, keeps a small corner whatever is inserted (the unreachable bound is the face's input angle); curved patches are not re-projected onto their true surface; the point count grows quickly on a shell with a very small angle (hence the budget and the opt-in); no Windows or hosted-CI evidence.
