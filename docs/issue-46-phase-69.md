# Issue 46 — Phase 69: flat-patch cleanup of a sampled shell (#52/#53)

New `solid_kernel::shell_patch::dissolve_flat(vertices, facets, options)` for any closed, outward-oriented, 2-manifold triangulated shell. It dissolves vertices that carry no geometry and re-triangulates the flat patch around them without adding a point:

- A *patch* is a set of triangles joined by flat edges (the smaller triangle's opposite vertex within `relative_tolerance` x bounding-box diagonal of the larger triangle's plane, on the far side of the edge, so a folded flap is a crease). Plane = the patch's largest triangle; a patch with a vertex off it is left alone.
- A vertex is dissolvable when it is interior to flat patches only, or lies on exactly two crease edges whose chord it is within `collinear_distance` x diagonal of and strictly between (a point on a straight feature line). Corners, bends and anything touching a curved neighbour stay.
- Each affected patch: boundary loops of the kept vertices (outer + holes, hole bridged to the outer ring), ear clipping choosing the best ear, then Lawson flips to the constrained Delaunay triangulation. No point is added; hole and outer boundary edges are constraints.
- Guards: a patch is re-triangulated only if its area is preserved (1e-9 relative plus the geometric tolerance times its perimeter) and its smallest corner angle does not fall below `min(old, acceptable_angle_deg = 10)`. A patch that fails keeps its vertices and the pass repeats (up to `max_rounds`). The whole result is verified: closed oriented manifold, signed volume preserved (tolerance scaled by the collinear distance times the surface area), no zero-area facet.
- Generic: no model-specific constants; the tolerances are relative to the bounding box. `step-inventory --clean-shell` applies it before `--conform` and prints the report plus the worst triangles before and after (`shell_patch::worst_triangles`).

**A defect avoided.** The first version re-triangulated unconditionally and made the sampled NEMA17 shell worse (smallest angle 0.0123 to 6.7e-6 degrees): dropping the collinear points between a needle's ends stretches the needle. The angle guard above is the fix, and `cleanup_never_makes_the_worst_triangle_worse` pins it.

Evidence (macOS arm64): 6 unit tests. A 4x3x2 gridded box collapses to 8 vertices / 12 facets with volume 24 and area 52 exact and angles >= atan(2/4) = 26.57 degrees (the best possible for those rectangles); a box with a point slid to 1e-4 from its edge end (minimum angle < 0.1 degrees) returns to 8/12 with >= 14.04 degrees; a square annulus prism (48 vertices / 96 facets) becomes 16 / 32 with the hole intact (volume 12, area 48 exact); an already-minimal box is bit-for-bit unchanged; an open shell is refused. `solid-kernel` suite (feature off): 194 passed, 0 failed; clippy clean for the new file. On the sampled bracket shell (used as an example only): 469 to 452 vertices, 978 to 944 facets, 25 patches re-triangulated, smallest angle unchanged at 0.0123 degrees (never worse).

Limits (not fixed): the needles that block `plc_conform` on the bracket remain. The measured worst triangles lie in flat faces and include a vertex 3.6e-6 mm off the chord of its neighbours; that vertex also belongs to curved (tangent-seam) triangles, so it cannot be dissolved by re-triangulating the flat patch. The next step this evidence points to is a manifold-safe weld of such near-degenerate seam vertices across both sides (a shell edge collapse with orientation, link-condition and tolerance guards). Curved patches are not remeshed; not wired into the production strategy (OCCT stays production); no Windows or hosted-CI evidence.
