# Issue 46 — Phase 82: held-out validation of the cost model (#60)

`fea-core/tests/cost_validation.rs` compares `meshing::cost` with the in-tree solver on meshes the model was not calibrated on (Quad8 plates, Hex8 blocks, a Tri6 plate with a hole; macOS arm64, release).

**Factor size (deterministic test, always run).** `MeshCost::of` predicts the Cholesky factor size from the mesh. Against the solver's `factor_nnz` the ratio was 0.98 to 1.20 on six meshes (the Hex8 blocks over-predict by 12-20 %: the cost mesh fixes one node, the solver a face).

**Predicted solve time (timing tests, `--ignored`).** Rates calibrated on one mesh and applied to another size of the same family: ratios (predicted / measured) from 0.20 (a 1.5k-dof mesh) through 1.4 to **7.4** (a 41k-dof hole plate calibrated on a 1.9k-dof one). The cause is visible in the stage table: factor time per `factor_nnz^1.5` falls monotonically with size (Quad8 1.1e-7 to 5.6e-9 ms, Hex8 1.9e-8 to 3.7e-9, hole plate 1.0e-7 to 7.1e-9 between 1e5 and 1e7 entries, a 20x drop), because the factorisation gets more efficient as fronts grow and carries a fixed overhead at small sizes; assembly per unit also falls (about 3x) and the solve per factor entry is roughly constant (1-2e-6 ms). A single-mesh calibration therefore extrapolates poorly in either direction; **calibrate on a mesh within a few times of the target size** (calibrated on a mid-size mesh of its own family the prediction stayed within about 0.5-2x; cross-family rates were off by up to 9x).

A power-law model fitted between two sizes (`ScalingCostModel`, written and measured here) was within 0.43-1.8x on 11 of 12 held-out meshes but its error was not clearly smaller than that of a single large-sample rate (0.16-2.8x), so it is **not shipped**: no evidenced improvement.

**Defect found and fixed: the iterative regime.** Above `AUTO_ITERATIVE_FACTOR_NNZ` (60 M) or `AUTO_ITERATIVE_DOFS` (150 k) a 3D system is solved with PCG + AMG (`SolveMethod::Auto`), whose `factor_nnz` is 0 and whose time is not the direct time. Measured: Hex8 26^3 (59k dofs, 54 M factor entries) factors in 1.9 s; Hex8 29^3 (81k dofs) and 32^3 (108k dofs, predicted 130 M entries) are solved iteratively in 0.9 s total. The model priced them as huge direct factorisations, so a time or memory limit in `choose_within` rejected or misjudged them. `MeshCost` gains `iterative` (same rule as `Auto`, `solved_iteratively`); `choose_within` with any limit now excludes an iterative candidate with an explicit reason instead of checking the limit against a model that does not describe the solve. Ranking without limits is unchanged. Tests: the rule at its thresholds (unit), the flag agreeing with the solver's actual choice at Hex8 22/26/29/32 and the `choose_within` rejection (ignored timing test).

Limits (not fixed): the iterative solve itself is not priced (it needs its own calibration: setup + iterations per dof, only two points measured here); the memory estimate for an iterative mesh is the direct figure, an upper bound; no contact/plasticity iteration model; single machine; no Windows or hosted-CI evidence. #60 stays open: accuracy is not modelled, and the rates remain machine-measured.
