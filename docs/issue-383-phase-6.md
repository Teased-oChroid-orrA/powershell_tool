2026-11-27. Item 2 remains open. The offline tapped contact pilot solves with length divided by pitch and stress divided by parent modulus; force scales back by modulus times pitch squared. Its three-mesh acceptance still used a fixed 0.000001 in penetration limit, and the elastic force-balance ratio used a 1 lbf denominator floor. Those checks could change their decision for a geometrically similar scaled problem.

Both elastic and plastic mesh gates now compare penetration divided by pitch with 0.000028. This equals the former 0.000001 in limit at 28 TPI. Both force checks use reaction ratios, with positive reaction already required by the solver. The existing doubled-length/halved-TPI three-mesh case passes: reaction scales by four, gap by two, and the mesh-change fraction stays the same. These changes make the acceptance criteria dimensionless; they do not change the finite-element equations or claim a measured capacity.

All 136 `fastened-joint-solver` tests passed after this gate change (`cargo test -p fastened-joint-solver --locked`).

The severe synthetic parent law (20,000 psi initial yield, 1,000 psi hardening slope) still does not qualify. At the requested 0.004 in travel, the original three-mesh run exhausted 200 step cuts after an invalid finite-strain material state in 86.6 s. Backtracking from invalid full Newton trials did not resolve it: the run again exhausted 200 cuts and took 240.2 s, so that kernel change was reverted. At 0.0025 in travel (0.07 pitch), an exploratory three-mesh run completed but differed 5.8% in response and 5.6% in per-turn load, exceeding the unchanged 2% gate. Its passing test was removed. The failed material update and mesh sensitivity cannot be identified as a physical collapse load without a converged response and independent material/failure evidence.

A focused two-mesh probe at 0.0025 in travel isolated the discrepancy. At edge sizes 0.1 and 0.07 pitch, reaction was 1,145.49 and 1,081.34 lbf; first-turn transfer was 886.14 and 825.05 lbf. The fixed 0.3-pitch parent gauge strain was 0.01105 and 0.01172, while the peak integration-point strain rose from 0.397 to 0.690. Yielded integration-point fractions were 31.4% and 26.6%. The local peak is strongly mesh-sensitive and the nonlocal response also misses the acceptance gate. These are dimensionless-solver outputs converted for the report, not physical measurements. The temporary probe was removed.

The Workbench continues to show analytical capacity only. The 1D elastic load-sharing diagnostic and offline 2D contact/plastic pilot do not supply plastic-collapse or progressive-stripping capacity. No nut fracture parameter, nominal S235/S355 curve, or fixed knockdown factor is transferred to the tapped parent. Before Tier A can produce a model-based limit, establish a stable load or residual-distortion observable across mesh and travel steps with a measured constitutive curve and supported boundary conditions. Tier B needs separately calibrated fracture and held-out physical tests.
