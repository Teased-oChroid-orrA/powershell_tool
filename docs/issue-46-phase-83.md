# Issue 46 — Phase 83: where constrained recovery stops terminating (#53, the 18 degree gate)

`solid-kernel/tests/plc_angle_sweep.rs` (sweeps are `--ignored`, release, `--no-default-features`) measures `plc_conform::conform` on generic families that are not the NEMA17 bracket: isosceles prisms with apex angle 60 to 4 degrees, square slabs of falling thickness (0.57 degrees at the thinnest), and surface-mesh-like slabs (an n x n grid top and bottom, one quad layer of wall, wall/face angle set by the thickness; n = 4 to 10), each with the shipped options and the missing-only heuristic, with and without `max_radius_edge = 2`.

Measured (macOS arm64):
- **Plain recovery** (what `NativeConstrained` is certified for) terminated on every prism (down to 4 degrees, 6 Steiner points), every coarse slab (down to 0.57 degrees, 5 points) and every surface-mesh-like slab tried (down to 5.71 degrees, 92 points). The smallest input angle alone does not predict failure of recovery on these inputs; the failures documented earlier (the 7-degree diagonal slab under refinement, the NEMA17 shell) are about graded, finely tessellated shells and quality refinement.
- **Radius-edge refinement** (`max_radius_edge = 2`) is where the angle matters, and it matters near the registry's number: on three grid sizes (6, 8, 10) with wall angle 14 to 24 degrees, refinement terminated at 22 and 24 degrees in all three, at 20 degrees in two of three, and **failed at 18 degrees and below in all three** (`Limit` after the 4000-point budget, 5-9 s each, or `Geometry`). No input below 22 degrees was uniformly refinable.

Consequences: (1) **the 18-degree gate is not relaxed**, and it is not conservative for quality refinement: 18 degrees passes the gate but failed in 3 of 3 refinement trials, so any request that asks the native strategy for a radius-edge bound must not be certified at 18 (the sweep puts that boundary at about 22 degrees on these families); the registry has no such request field, and `NativeConstrained`'s claim is recovery only, so nothing changes in `compatibility.rs`. (2) For recovery alone the gate is conservative on simple shells; relaxing it would need a corpus of real shells (ABC, `issue-46-benchmarks.md` section C) because the measured failures are density- and grading-related, not angle-related; synthetic families cannot justify a new constant. (3) Bracket work should therefore target grading/sliver structure of the shell (what `prepare_shell` cleans), not the gate.

Limits: three synthetic families, one machine, one quality bound (2); a real-shell corpus was not run; no Windows or hosted-CI evidence.
