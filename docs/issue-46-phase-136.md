# Phase 136 — one vertex per rim point in the snap: tried and rejected (2026-10-22)

Epic #46 / #63, continuing [Phase 134](issue-46-phase-134.md) and [Phase 135](issue-46-phase-135.md). Candidate fix for the boss-rim scaled-Jacobian defect: in `tetmesh`'s snap loop, a boundary vertex whose feature target (the rim) lies within a fraction `K` of its local edge from an already-placed neighbouring boundary vertex takes the plain surface projection instead of the rim, so two vertices are not pulled onto one rim point; optionally only at sharp features (normals more than 30 degrees apart).

Result on `plate with a boss` (`occt_vs_native` table, release, default size): with `K = 0.75`, OCCT 6 to 0 and native 20 to 0 tets under the 0.1 gate (minimum scaled Jacobian 0.063 to 0.120); across the 25-row table every row passed the gate on both paths and the benchmark solves agreed (box 0.0 %, cylinder -2.6 % energy as before, boss -4 %, fillets <0.1 %). `K = 0.2` left 3 native tets, `K = 0.4` and `0.55` left 4 and 5.

**Rejected anyway: it degrades curved-body accuracy.** `fea-core/tests/high_order_convergence.rs` (cylinder, 672-2852 tets) at `K = 0.75`, rms radial error against the closed form, baseline to candidate: Tet10 h 0.40 `6.5e-3` to `4.0e-2`, h 0.28 `1.1e-3` to `2.1e-2`; Tet4 h 0.40 `3.2e-2` to `3.6e-2`, h 0.28 `1.1e-2` to `2.6e-2`; maximum radial error Tet10 `1.9e-3` to `3.3e-2`. The test's ordering assertion (Tet10 below Tet4) fails at h 0.40. It also fails at `K = 0.4` and `0.55`; only `K = 0.2` passes, and that leaves 3 native tets under the gate. Without the sharp-feature restriction the filleted-box rim tagging test (`fillet_rim_tagging`, tagged area 73.3 against exact 80) also failed; the restriction fixed that one only. A second test, `tetmesh_repair::every_element_of_a_curved_solid_passes_the_gate_after_the_repair`, lost its premise (the unrepaired cylinder no longer had elements under the gate).

Conclusion: stopping rim snapping for vertices near another rim vertex trades a few sub-gate boss elements for a large loss of boundary fidelity on curved bodies with sharp rims; the repair of the snap must be topological, as Phase 134 argued (collapse of the short rim edge with the wall vertex moved onto the rim point, preceded by flips that remove the link-condition obstruction), not a restriction of the snap. Reverted; nothing retained. The same boss-rim defect stays: 6 (OCCT) and 20 (native) tets under 0.1 on `plate with a boss`, all other rows of the table pass the gate on both paths.
